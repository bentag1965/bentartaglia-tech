AI Experiment Report — November 1, 2026

Ben Tartaglia · Progress report · Data checked November 1, 2026

The Lab has moved beyond its first two checkpoint days. Four cohorts now contain 289 stored responses, including 241 collected since the October 2 report. That is useful progress, but the coverage is uneven: 35 observation slots are partial, and the review queue has grown. This update follows the evidence through both.

Question

How consistently do six AI providers follow instructions, handle assumptions, and produce useful answers when controlled prompts are repeated over time? The reporting question this month is narrower: what was collected, where is coverage incomplete, and how much review remains?

Method

The first cohort, started September 27, uses the bridge-expansion task described in the baseline report. Its supplied reference calculation remains 36 millimeters. Three additional cohorts began October 18, October 25, and November 1. They address verification of a hypothetical breaking-news claim, a constrained shift-allocation problem, and a launch forecast with explicit uncertainty. The forecasting task supplies assumptions for an exercise; it is not a report of business results.

Each cohort uses fresh sessions at minute 0, 5, 15, and 30 for checkpoint days 0, 1, 7, 14, 21, 30, 90, 180, and 365. The six participating providers remain OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI, Mistral, and DeepSeek. Different cohort prompts must be analyzed separately before drawing any claim about behavior over time.

Change since the previous report

The snapshot grew from one cohort and 48 responses to four cohorts and 289 responses. New prompt domains broaden the investigation, while incomplete collection introduces a comparison problem: we no longer have equal coverage across providers. Any later analysis must account for which prompt, checkpoint, and model actually produced each sample.

The current cohorts each reference prompt version 2, and the stored rubric remains version 1. Two evaluator model identifiers appear: gpt-6-astra and claude-opus-5-5. The October 2 aggregate snapshot did not preserve prompt hashes or evaluator/model identifiers, so this report cannot establish whether those configurations changed within the reporting interval. This is a provenance limit to address, not evidence of stability.

Review status

There are 532 evaluation records: 382 completed, 143 failed, and seven marked as requiring human review. Compared with the baseline, that is 348 additional completed records, 143 additional failed records, and five additional review-required records. No human reviews are recorded.

These are evaluation-record counts, not distinct responses certified as correct. The records refer to 20 distinct source responses and 266 distinct comparison responses; those populations can overlap. Multiple evaluations can concern the same response. A completed automated evaluation does not represent Ben’s approval, and a failed record does not by itself establish that an answer was wrong.

Limitations

This report audits execution, coverage, and review metadata. It does not adjudicate all raw responses, validate the evaluator’s scores, or establish model rankings, correctness rates, or drift findings. Four prompt tasks still cannot represent broad capability. Uneven provider coverage, partial checkpoints, failed evaluation records, and outstanding human reviews constrain interpretation.

Oracle Table debates have a separate release and approval workflow. Their publication does not resolve this repeated-question study’s review flags, and their outputs are excluded from the counts here.

Leadership implications

The operating lesson is concrete: a healthy scheduler can coexist with incomplete evidence. A useful dashboard needs to show expected samples, collected samples, partial work, and review obligations alongside its green status indicators.

As a research system expands, the denominator matters more. “289 responses collected” is informative; “289 of 336 expected samples in due slots” makes the gap actionable. Before using these results to change an operational workflow, I would want the missing coverage understood and the flagged comparisons reviewed.
