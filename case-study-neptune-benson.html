Case Study · IT Management & Modernization

Coordinating multi-site upgrades and acquisition integration.

At Neptune Benson, managing IT meant more than keeping systems running. It meant getting the right people, equipment, vendors, and timelines together so infrastructure work could be delivered across locations.

Professional ProfileView Résumé

Leadership scope

3geographically distributed direct reports
$150Ktechnology budget

The challenge

Multi-site work depends on coordination, not just technical skill.

Hardware and services upgrades required planning across locations and people with different capabilities and workloads. A successful upgrade depended on equipment arriving, the right technical resources being assigned, and travel and work schedules aligning with site needs.

People and coverage

Managed a team of three technicians distributed geographically. Arranged additional technical coverage when local expertise or workload required more than one person.

Budget and logistics

Managed an approximately $150,000 IT budget focused on hardware replacements and services. Coordinated equipment delivery, travel, lodging, vehicles, and site scheduling as part of execution readiness.

Business changes

Supported technology work associated with a purchase/integration, including network readdressing and a new phone-system transition.

Approach

Make the next upgrade predictable.

I approached site upgrades as an operational delivery problem: define the scope, verify parts and equipment, assess skills and staffing, coordinate travel and timing, and make sure the onsite workload matched the people assigned. When necessary, two or more technicians worked together to reduce the risk of an overloaded or under-resourced site visit.

For acquisition integration, technical changes such as IP addressing and telephony required coordination with the broader transition rather than being treated as isolated configuration tasks.

Leadership takeaway

Technical judgment with management accountability.

This experience connects the operational details of infrastructure engineering with formal responsibility for people, spending, project coordination, vendor/services delivery, and business continuity. The goal was not simply to complete a technical task; it was to make distributed work achievable and supportable.

Scope figures reflect budget and staffing responsibility, not claimed savings or unverified project outcomes.
