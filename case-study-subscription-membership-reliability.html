Building a Reliable Subscription-to-Membership System

A creator-led digital membership platform needed more than checkout. Payments had to translate into the right access, billing events needed to reconcile cleanly, and failures needed a safe path through retries and review.

The Problem

A payment is only the beginning of the workflow.

A paid membership spans several systems: checkout and recurring billing, account identity, protected content, event processing, and customer communication. A successful transaction can still leave a gap if its event is delayed, repeated, or recorded differently downstream.

The goal was to connect those systems while making the state of each handoff visible—and to handle billing problems without allowing an uncertain event to make an irreversible access decision.

What I Built

Connect the customer path to an operational system.

Payment to Access

Connected recurring payment events to membership records and entitlement synchronization, with reconciliation between the payment processor and the access layer.

Subscription Self-Service

Added a secure billing portal flow so members could reach subscription management through their signed-in account.

Event Processing

Designed bounded queue processing with duplicate handling, trusted event lookups, claim-token checks, retry limits, and a review path for incomplete or contradictory evidence.

Safety and Reliability

Automate the evidence work. Keep consequential decisions controlled.

Billing events were treated as evidence to verify, not commands to execute blindly. The design checks trusted event records, preserves processing state, and distinguishes transient provider errors from evidence that needs human review.

The negative-billing worker was designed to record assessments without issuing entitlement changes, customer notices, or access-removal instructions. That boundary made it possible to test classification and retry behavior while keeping customer-impact actions behind a separate release and acceptance process.

Validation

Exercise the failure paths, not only the happy path.

Adapter Checks

304 of 304 local synthetic and mocked checks passed.

Worker Scenarios

13 of 13 combined mocked queue-worker scenarios passed.

Database Queue

58 of 58 queue checks passed in each of two isolated database runs.

These figures describe local, mocked, and isolated validation. They do not represent a completed hosted, end-to-end acceptance of the negative-billing lifecycle.

Outcome and Status

A stronger payment foundation, with honest release boundaries.

The core payment path was verified through successful payment, event delivery, reconciliation, and entitlement synchronization. A member-facing billing portal also opened successfully. The work established a clearer operating model for subscription events and a tested foundation for safer failure handling.

End-to-end acceptance of failed-payment recovery and the resulting membership-access transition remains a separate gate. Production customer notices and automatic paid-access removal remain disabled until that flow is reviewed and accepted.

What This Demonstrates

Systems Integration

Connecting billing, identity, membership, data, and communication layers into one supportable customer journey.

Operational Judgment

Separating verified facts from incomplete evidence and giving consequential actions an explicit decision boundary.

Failure-Aware Design

Planning for duplicate delivery, retries, provider errors, backlog, recovery, and manual review before the system depends on them.

The project reinforced a principle I use across infrastructure and automation: the system should make its state visible, preserve evidence, and give people a safe way to intervene when the evidence is not clear.
