Research Method

A simple discipline: define the question, preserve the evidence, and make the assumptions visible.

1. Question

Start with a specific technical or operational question instead of a predetermined conclusion.

2. Method

Document the environment, test conditions, data sources, assumptions, controls, and limitations.

3. Evidence

Preserve observations, outputs, logs, measurements, version information, and enough context to revisit the result later.

4. Interpretation

Separate what was observed from what is inferred, and note where stronger evidence would change the conclusion.

Research Standard

Exploration is allowed to be incomplete. Claims are not.

Research notes on this site will identify whether something is a hypothesis, observation, working interpretation, or supported conclusion. Where results depend on a specific model version, configuration, environment, data set, or test window, that context should travel with the result.

I am less interested in being first to a conclusion than in being able to explain why I reached it—and what would make me revise it.

Publication Pattern

Three layers, depending on maturity.

Research Notes

Early observations, test designs, questions, unexpected results, and methodological decisions. Useful but explicitly provisional.

Technical Briefs

More structured findings with methodology, evidence, limitations, interpretation, and practical implications.

Reference Implementations

Public code or architecture showing how a finding, pattern, or operating principle can be translated into a working system.

Boundary

Curiosity without leaking somebody else’s system.

Research published here is based on public information, personal experimentation, sanitized reference architectures, and generalized technical patterns. Employer, customer, tenant, credential, device, and proprietary data stay out of the public research layer.
